# Coral Reefs and Coral Bleaching

Corals are animals, but most of their energy comes from microscopic algae called zooxanthellae that live inside their tissues. The algae photosynthesize and share sugars with the coral, and in return the coral provides a protected home and the nutrients the algae need.

When water becomes too warm for too long, corals expel their algae and turn white. This is called coral bleaching. A bleached coral is not dead, but it starves and dies if the stress continues for weeks or months.

Reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor, yet they support roughly a quarter of all known marine species. They also shelter coastlines from waves, which is why their loss affects fisheries and coastal communities.
