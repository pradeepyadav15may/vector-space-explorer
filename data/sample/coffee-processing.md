# Coffee Processing: Washed Versus Natural

After harvest, the seeds inside coffee cherries must be separated from the fruit. In the washed process, the pulp is removed mechanically and the seeds are fermented in water before drying. This usually gives a cleaner cup with brighter acidity.

In the natural process, the whole cherry is dried in the sun with the fruit still around the seed. The sugars from the fruit soak into the bean, which tends to produce fruitier flavours and a heavier body.

Honey processing falls between the two, with some pulp left on the seed during drying. Each method is a trade-off between clarity, sweetness and the risk of defects, and farmers choose based on climate and the buyer's preferences.
