# The Transistor: How Small Switches Changed Computing

In December 1947, researchers at Bell Labs demonstrated the first point-contact transistor. It could amplify and switch electrical signals using a solid semiconductor, which was a major step beyond the glass vacuum tube.

Transistors were smaller, cheaper, more reliable and used far less power than vacuum tubes. Within about a decade they had replaced tubes in radios and computers, and they made portable electronics practical.

Later, engineers learned to build many transistors on a single piece of silicon. That idea, the integrated circuit, is the reason a modern phone can contain billions of transistors.
