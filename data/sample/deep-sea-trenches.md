# The Deep Sea and Hydrothermal Vents

The Mariana Trench is the deepest known part of the ocean, reaching roughly 11 kilometres at the Challenger Deep. The pressure there is more than a thousand times the pressure at the surface, yet animals such as amphipods and snailfish live in the trench.

Hydrothermal vents form where seawater seeps through the crust, is heated by magma and returns as mineral-rich fluid. Bacteria there make energy from chemicals such as hydrogen sulfide instead of sunlight, a process called chemosynthesis.

Giant tube worms, which have no mouth or stomach, depend on chemosynthetic bacteria living inside their bodies. Vent communities show that life can thrive without any light from the Sun.
