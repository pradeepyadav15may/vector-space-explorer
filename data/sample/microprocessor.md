# The Microprocessor: A Computer on One Chip

The Intel 4004, released in 1971, is widely regarded as the first commercially available microprocessor. It was a 4-bit processor built from about 2,300 transistors and was designed for a calculator.

A microprocessor puts the central processing unit of a computer onto a single chip. Once that was possible, the cost of computing fell rapidly, which made personal computers and eventually embedded systems in cars, appliances and phones.

Each generation of processors packed more transistors into the same area, a trend described by Gordon Moore in 1965 and often called Moore's law. Its pace has slowed in recent years, but the idea shaped decades of planning in the industry.
