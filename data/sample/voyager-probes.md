# Voyager: Probes That Left the Solar System

Voyager 1 launched on 5 September 1977 and became the most distant human-made object in space. In 2012 it crossed the heliopause, the boundary where the Sun's solar wind gives way to the interstellar medium.

Each Voyager carries a golden phonograph record with sounds and images of Earth, intended as a message for any finder. The probes are powered by radioisotope generators whose output shrinks every year, so instruments are switched off one by one to save power.

Signals from Voyager 1 take more than a day to reach Earth. Engineers still send commands to it and receive data using the large antennas of the Deep Space Network.
