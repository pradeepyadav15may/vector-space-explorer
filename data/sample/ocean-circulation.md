# Ocean Circulation and the Global Conveyor Belt

Ocean water moves in a global circulation driven by wind and by differences in temperature and salt content. In the North Atlantic, cold and salty water becomes dense and sinks, feeding a slow deep current that carries water around the globe.

The Gulf Stream is a fast, warm current on the surface of the western Atlantic. It carries heat from the tropics northward, which keeps much of western Europe milder than other places at the same latitude.

The ocean has absorbed most of the extra heat added to the climate system since the mid twentieth century. Changes in circulation, especially any slowdown of the Atlantic overturning, could alter regional weather patterns.
