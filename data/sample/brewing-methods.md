# Brewing Methods: Espresso, Pour-Over and French Press

Espresso forces hot water at roughly nine bar of pressure through finely ground coffee in about 25 to 30 seconds. The high pressure extracts oils and dissolved solids quickly, producing a concentrated shot with a layer of crema.

Pour-over brewing sends hot water through a bed of medium-ground coffee in a filter. The brewer controls the pour rate, which shapes the clarity and sweetness of the cup, and the paper filter removes most oils and sediment.

A French press steeps coarse coffee in hot water for about four minutes before a metal filter presses the grounds down. Because the metal filter lets oils through, the result has a heavier body than a pour-over.

Grind size matters for every method. Finer grounds have more surface area, so they extract faster, and an extraction that is too fast or too slow tastes sour or bitter.
