# Where Coffee Comes From

Coffee comes from the seeds of Coffea plants, which grow in the tropics. The two species that matter most commercially are Coffea arabica, which is native to the highlands of Ethiopia, and Coffea canephora, known as robusta.

Arabica grows at higher altitudes, has more complex flavours and contains less caffeine. Robusta is hardier, resists disease better and contains roughly twice as much caffeine, which makes it more bitter.

Coffee was being cultivated and traded in Yemen by the fifteenth century. From there it spread through the Arab world, Europe and then to plantations in Asia, Africa and the Americas.
