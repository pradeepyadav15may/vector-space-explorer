# ENIAC: An Early General-Purpose Electronic Computer

ENIAC was completed in 1945 at the University of Pennsylvania and is often described as one of the first general-purpose electronic digital computers. It was built to calculate artillery firing tables for the US Army.

ENIAC used about 17,000 vacuum tubes and filled a room. It was programmed by setting switches and connecting cables by hand, so changing the task could take days of work. Its speed, though, was far beyond any mechanical calculator of the time.

The machine's designers, including John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert, went on to build commercial computers. Lessons from ENIAC about programming and reliability shaped the stored-program machines that followed.
