# Coffee Roasting: From Green Bean to Brown

Green coffee beans have little flavour until they are roasted. Heat drives the Maillard reaction and caramelization, which create the aromas and colours people associate with coffee.

At around 196 degrees Celsius inside the bean, the beans crack audibly as moisture and gas escape. This first crack is a common marker for light roasts. Continuing into a dark roast darkens the beans and pushes forward the flavours of smoke and bitterness.

Lighter roasts keep more of the origin's character, such as fruit or floral notes. Darker roasts hide some of that character behind roast flavours, which is why a roaster's choice of profile shapes the final cup as much as the farm does.
