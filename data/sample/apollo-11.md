# Apollo 11 and the First Moon Landing

On 20 July 1969, the Apollo 11 lunar module Eagle touched down in the Sea of Tranquility. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin were the two astronauts who landed on the Moon, while Michael Collins stayed in lunar orbit aboard the command module Columbia.

Armstrong stepped onto the surface first. The two astronauts spent about two and a half hours outside the lunar module, planting a flag, setting up experiments and collecting roughly 21 kilograms of rock and soil samples.

The mission showed that crewed landings were possible within the decade target set by President Kennedy in 1961. Its samples are still studied by scientists today, because few have been returned from anywhere beyond Earth.
