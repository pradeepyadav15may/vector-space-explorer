# Origins of the Internet and the Web

The ARPANET, funded by the US Department of Defense, sent its first message in October 1969 between computers at UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute. The network was designed to keep communication working even when parts of it failed.

In 1983 the network adopted the TCP/IP protocols, which let many different networks interconnect. This shared set of rules is the foundation of what people now call the internet.

In 1989, Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN. His design combined hypertext, unique addresses for documents and a protocol for requesting pages, which made the internet usable by people who were not specialists.
